EVIDENCE / COMPARISON

Published research vs Sentinel-4337

Reported F1 scores from two research papers alongside your saved benchmark. Different datasets and tasks: this is research context, not a head-to-head ranking.

F1-score comparison ⓘ

⚠   Important: Both papers detect malicious or phishing Ethereum accounts. Sentinel's benchmark separates unmodified background features from controlled synthetic perturbations—not verified real attacks.

Why our approach is different

These studies answer related but different security questions. The numbers cannot be used to claim Sentinel outperforms either paper.

DiT-SGCR · 2025

Detect malicious accounts

Data: labeled Ethereum phishing / transaction-network datasets, including MulDiGraph and B4E.

Features: directed, timestamped account-to-account graph interactions and global clusters.

Method: temporal structural embeddings, clustering, then Random Forest classification.

FAAN-GBM · 2024

Detect phishing nodes

Data: labeled Ethereum phishing account datasets from Etherscan and XBlock.

Features: node basics, transaction values, neighbor interactions and attention.

Method: feature enhancement, autoencoder and gradient boosting.

SENTINEL-4337

Evaluate ERC-4337 operation patterns

Data: real multichain UserOperation background + controlled synthetic feature perturbations.

Features: 17 trace-derived counts, flags and log-transformed counts.

Method: Random Forest advisory + separate Solidity deterministic policy.

Why controlled synthetic perturbations?

01 · JiffyScan access problems

In the project record, getUserOp returned HTTP 502, while getLatestBundles returned empty lists for tested networks. The attempted collection did not supply the required labeled malicious ERC-4337 examples.

02 · Different target data

The papers study labeled phishing accounts and transaction graphs. Sentinel needs ERC-4337 UserOperation trace features, which are not equivalent to paper datasets.

03 · Reproducible controlled evaluation

We retained real operation-derived background and injected controlled feature changes to test whether the model detects them, without inventing verified attack labels.

04 · Important limitation

A perturbed record is not a proven attack. The reported 94.17% F1 describes this controlled benchmark only, not deployment accuracy.
